# Frozen parametric illusion benchmark

## Status and contract

`COMPLETE`. All 22 frozen Canonical V1, center-surround LN, and causal CNN checkpoints were evaluated without training. Inputs use the same 17×17 L+M contract, all-zero observed spike history, 150 Hz time axis, and mean paired logit over 300–400 ms. Canonical additionally uses frozen `H1_off`, `direct_BC_off`, and `AC_off` structural clamps. The original SBC `(contrast=0.25, radius=8 px)` and Mach `(contrast=0.25, ramp width=8 px)` inputs and Canonical logits replay bitwise exactly (`max |error|=0`).

SBC uses contrast magnitudes `{0, .0625, .125, .25, .375, .5}` and Chebyshev context radii `{0,2,4,6,8}` px around a zero-valued 3×3 target. Radius 0 is the exact zero-context control. Mach uses the same contrasts and widths `{0,2,4,8,12,16}` px; width 0 is the no-ramp step-edge control. Pixel pitch is `0.05390625 deg/px`.

At every nonzero SBC extent, the Canonical and LN cohort curves decrease strictly with contrast, while CNN increases strictly. For Mach, Canonical and LN are strictly decreasing for the dark endpoint and strictly increasing for the bright endpoint at all six widths. CNN is strictly increasing for the dark endpoint at all widths; its bright curve is strictly increasing only at widths 8/12/16 and is non-monotone or decreasing at widths 0/2/4.

At the single-cell level, all 88 nonzero-extent SBC contrast curves are strictly monotone for each model. Canonical has 132/132 strictly monotone dark and 132/132 bright Mach contrast curves; LN has 132/132 and 126/132; CNN has 111/132 and 103/132. Full direction, endpoint change, monotonic fraction, and Spearman values are in the monotonicity tables.

## Between-model differences

Across 80 non-control grid points, Canonical−LN bootstrap CIs include zero at all 80 points. Canonical−CNN differs at 26/80 points: 18/30 Mach-dark and 8/30 Mach-bright points are negative; 0/20 SBC points are resolved. Mean absolute cohort differences are:

| Comparison | SBC | Mach dark | Mach bright |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Canonical−LN | 0.015492 | 0.006220 | 0.006646 |
| Canonical−CNN | 0.208424 | 0.099166 | 0.021410 |

Therefore, the Canonical model does **not** show a systematic parametric response absent from both baselines: it differs from CNN on part of the Mach grid but is not resolved from LN anywhere on the non-control grid.

## Canonical clamp effects

Point counts refer to bootstrap CIs excluding zero across non-control settings.

| Clamp off−normal | SBC | Mach dark | Mach bright |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| H1-off | 0/20 | 0/30 | 0/30 |
| direct-BC-off | 0/20 | 0/30 | 0/30 |
| AC-off | 0/20 | 30/30 positive | 25/30 negative |

For Mach bright, the five unresolved points are exactly the width-0 no-ramp controls; all widths 2–16 are resolved. At `C=.5, W=8`, AC-off changes dark by `+0.012761 [0.000722, 0.024392]` and bright by `−0.012761 [−0.024392, −0.000722]`. At `C=.5, R=8`, the SBC AC-off change is `+0.083860 [−0.002749, 0.168405]` and is not cohort-resolved.

Thus AC-off selectively shifts essentially the full nonzero-width Mach parameter surface relative to H1-off and direct-BC-off, but this does not extend to a cohort-resolved SBC surface.

The AC-off surface is strongly structured in individual cells rather than generated by uniformly weak effects: 14/22 cells have positive whole-surface cosine with the cohort and 8/22 have negative cosine, with median absolute cosine `0.998–1.000`. At `C=.5, W=8`, Mach-dark has 14 positive/8 negative cell effects and Mach-bright has 8 positive/14 negative; SBC likewise has 14 positive/8 negative. Cohort direction therefore reflects averaging of two opposite single-cell directions and their unequal 14/8 composition.

## Position relative to natural-movie training inputs

The range calculation uses the three unique frozen full-training stimulus tensors underlying the 22 fits; duplicate cell presentations are removed (`33,600` frames, `9,710,400` scalar values).

- Natural scalar range: `[-0.824550, 12.601489]`; all probe scalar levels are inside it.
- Probe extremes `−0.5` and `+0.5` lie at natural scalar percentiles `21.12%` and `65.87%`; zero lies at `47.16%`.
- All probe frame means/minima/maxima remain inside the corresponding natural-frame min/max range.
- `68/166` probe patches have spatial standard deviation below the natural-frame minimum and `78/166` have RMS below the natural-frame minimum. These are chiefly zero/uniform controls and low-energy small-context patches.

The sweep is therefore not scalar-amplitude out-of-range, but part of it is out-of-range in per-frame spatial variance/energy. This is a range statement only; it does not establish distance from the natural-movie manifold.
